Anatomy of a wave packet

A wave packet is a point that spins around a centre while its distance from that centre swells, then fades. Turn the diagram and the same object becomes a circle, a cosine and a sine.

Two ingredients

Multiply a slow bell by a fast spin and you get the packet. Move the sliders and watch every plot above change.

The envelope

e−γ(t−t₀)²

A bell curve centred on t₀. It decides when the packet is loud and when it has faded.

×

The spin

eiωt = cos ωt + i sin ωt

A point lapping the unit circle. Its shadows on the two axes are the cosine and the sine.

Multiply the two and the point spirals outward while the bell rises, then back inward as it falls. That path is the helix in the main diagram.

Try this

  • Drag the main diagram until the helix looks like a plain circle. You are now looking straight down the time axis.

  • Shrink the width. The packet fades sooner, so fewer loops are visible. A packet that lasts only a few laps cannot have a perfectly sharp pitch, and that trade-off is the heart of the uncertainty principle.

  • Double the spin rate. The loops tighten while the envelope stays exactly where it was.

T is one lap at a spin rate of 1 lap per T. Time runs from 0 to 6 T and the packet is centred at t₀ = 3 T.
